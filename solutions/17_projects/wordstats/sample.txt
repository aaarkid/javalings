The lighthouse keeper climbed the stairs every evening. Every evening the
same one hundred and twelve steps, the same cold rail under his hand, the
same lamp waiting at the top.

He did not mind the climb. The climb was the job, and the job was the light,
and the light was for the ships. Ships he would never see, on nights he
would never remember, carrying people who would never know his name.

Some evenings the fog came in so thick that the light seemed to stop a few
metres past the glass. He lit it anyway. A light that nobody sees is still
a light, he told the gulls, and the gulls, as always, said nothing useful.
